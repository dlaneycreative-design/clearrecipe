Frijoles Charros

Cowboy beans simmered with bacon, chorizo, and pico de gallo in a savory broth. Smoky, hearty, and made for a crowd.

Prep 15 minsCook 2 hrsServes 8
#Mexican#Mains

The whole recipe in 60 seconds

Frijoles Charros, in 5 ideas

  1. Charro means cowboy, and these beans are ranch style party food
  2. The bean cooking liquid is liquid gold; never drain it
  3. Chorizo and bacon make this rich enough to be a meal on its own
  4. The fresh pico stirred in at the end keeps it bright
  5. These taste even better the next day as the flavors meld

What you need

Ingredients

  • 1 lb dried pinto beans
  • 6 slices bacon, diced
  • 1/2 lb Mexican chorizo
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 2 tomatoes, diced
  • 2 jalapenos, diced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 bunch cilantro, chopped
  • Salt to taste

How to make it

Directions

  1. Simmer the beans in water for 1.5 hours until tender.
  2. Fry the bacon until crisp, then brown the chorizo in the same pot.
  3. Add onion, tomatoes, jalapenos, and garlic. Cook 5 minutes.
  4. Add the beans with their cooking liquid to the pot.
  5. Simmer 30 minutes until the broth thickens slightly.
  6. Stir in cilantro and serve with warm tortillas.
Money saver. Dried beans cost pennies per serving compared to canned and the texture is far superior; cook a big pot on the weekend.
