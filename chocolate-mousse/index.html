Chocolate Mousse

Impossibly light and deeply dark, this mousse is pure chocolate velvet in a glass. Three ingredients, a little patience, and a spoon are all you need.

Prep 20 minsCook 0 minsServes 6
#French#Desserts

The whole recipe in 60 seconds

Chocolate Mousse, in 5 ideas

  1. Whipping the cream to soft peaks only is the secret to a cloudlike texture
  2. Cooling the melted chocolate slightly keeps the yolks from scrambling
  3. Folding gently protects the air you whipped in, which is what makes mousse airy
  4. A pinch of salt sharpens the chocolate flavor without making it salty
  5. Chilling overnight deepens the flavor and sets the texture perfectly

What you need

Ingredients

  • 200g dark chocolate (70% cocoa), chopped
  • 4 large eggs, separated
  • 240ml heavy cream
  • 1 pinch fine sea salt
  • 1 tbsp granulated sugar
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • Cocoa powder, for dusting

How to make it

Directions

  1. Melt the chopped chocolate in a heatproof bowl set over a pan of barely simmering water, stirring until smooth. Remove from heat and let cool 5 minutes until warm but not hot.
  2. Whisk the egg yolks, one at a time, into the warm chocolate along with the vanilla extract until glossy and combined.
  3. In a clean bowl, beat the egg whites with the salt until soft peaks form. Sprinkle in the sugar and beat until firm, glossy peaks hold.
  4. In another bowl, whip the heavy cream until it forms soft, billowy peaks that just barely hold their shape.
  5. Fold one third of the whipped cream into the chocolate mixture to loosen it, then gently fold in the rest.
  6. Fold the beaten egg whites into the chocolate cream in two additions, using a spatula and slow, wide strokes, until no streaks remain. Do not overmix or the mousse will deflate.
  7. Spoon into six small glasses or ramekins, cover, and chill at least 4 hours or overnight.
  8. Dust lightly with cocoa powder just before serving.
Buy supermarket own-brand 70% dark chocolate rather than a fancy bar. It melts beautifully and costs half as much, and once whipped into mousse nobody can tell the difference.
