Quenelles

Cloud light dumplings of pike or chicken mousseline, poached and baked in a creamy lobster sauce. Lyon's most famous dish.

Prep 45 minsCook 30 minsServes 4
#French#Mains

The whole recipe in 60 seconds

Quenelles, in 5 ideas

  1. Everything must stay cold or the mousseline will break and turn grainy
  2. The two spoon shaping technique takes practice; wet spoons are the secret
  3. Quenelles should feel like savory clouds, never dense
  4. The classic sauce Nantua is made with crayfish
  5. Chicken quenelles are easier for beginners and just as delicious

What you need

Ingredients

  • 1 lb white fish fillets (pike or sole), or ground chicken
  • 2 egg whites
  • 1 cup heavy cream, chilled
  • 1/2 cup panade (bread soaked in milk, squeezed dry)
  • Salt, pepper, and nutmeg
  • 2 cups fish stock
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 2 tbsp flour
  • 1 cup cream sauce or bisque, for baking

How to make it

Directions

  1. Blend the fish with the panade, egg whites, salt, pepper, and nutmeg into a smooth paste.
  2. Beat in the cold cream a little at a time until light and fluffy. Chill 30 minutes.
  3. Shape into ovals with two spoons dipped in hot water.
  4. Poach in simmering stock 8 minutes until they float and firm up.
  5. Place in a buttered baking dish, cover with cream sauce.
  6. Bake at 400 F for 15 minutes until golden and puffed.
Money saver. Make a double batch and freeze the shaped raw quenelles; poach them straight from frozen when you want an impressive dinner.
