Cataplana

The Algarve's clam-shaped copper pot gives its name to this seafood stew: fish, clams, and chouriço steamed with peppers and wine.

Prep 25 minsCook 25 minsServes 4
#Portuguese#Mains

The whole recipe in 60 seconds

Cataplana, in 5 ideas

  1. A tight lid is essential; the steam does all the work
  2. Layer the seafood so everything cooks evenly
  3. Discard any clams that do not open after cooking
  4. The chouriço adds a smoky depth you will not get from seafood alone
  5. Mop up the broth with bread; it is the best part

What you need

Ingredients

  • 1 lb firm white fish fillets, cut into chunks
  • 1 lb littleneck clams, scrubbed
  • 1/2 lb shrimp, peeled
  • 1/4 lb chouriço, diced
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 1 red bell pepper, sliced
  • 1 green bell pepper, sliced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 cup dry white wine
  • 1 cup fish stock
  • 1/4 cup olive oil
  • Fresh cilantro and lemon for serving

How to make it

Directions

  1. Heat the olive oil in a wide heavy pot or cataplana and brown the chouriço until it releases its fat.
  2. Add the onion, peppers, and garlic and cook until softened, about 8 minutes.
  3. Pour in the wine and stock and bring to a simmer.
  4. Nestle in the fish chunks, then scatter the clams and shrimp over the top.
  5. Cover tightly and steam 10 to 12 minutes until the clams open and the fish is opaque.
  6. Discard any unopened clams, shower with cilantro, and serve with lemon and crusty bread.
Money saver. Buy whatever firm fish is on sale; the stew is forgiving and the broth unifies everything.
