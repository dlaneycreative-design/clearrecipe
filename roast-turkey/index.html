Roast Turkey

A golden, juicy bird with crisp skin and plenty of drippings for gravy. This is the centerpiece the whole table gathers around.

Prep 30 minsCook 3 hrs 30 minsServes 10 to 12
#American#Mains

The whole recipe in 60 seconds

Roast Turkey, in 5 ideas

  1. Dry skin is the secret to crisp skin, so pat the bird down well and leave it uncovered in the fridge the night before if you can.
  2. Herb butter under the skin bastes the breast from the inside as it roasts.
  3. A thermometer takes all the guessing out. Thigh at 165°F means done, never dry.
  4. Resting is not optional. The juices settle back into the meat instead of running onto the board.
  5. Room temperature stock in the pan keeps the drippings golden instead of scorched.

What you need

Ingredients

  • 1 whole turkey (12 to 14 lb), thawed
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 tbsp kosher salt
  • 2 tsp black pepper
  • 2 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tbsp fresh sage, chopped
  • 1 tbsp fresh thyme leaves
  • 1 tbsp fresh rosemary, chopped
  • 1 onion, quartered
  • 1 lemon, halved
  • 4 cups turkey or chicken stock
  • Kitchen twine

How to make it

Directions

  1. Take the turkey out of the fridge 1 hour before roasting. Remove the neck and giblets, pat the bird very dry with paper towels, and let it sit at room temperature.
  2. Heat the oven to 325°F. Mash the softened butter with the salt, pepper, garlic powder, and chopped herbs until it forms a paste.
  3. Loosen the skin over the breast with your fingers and spread half the herb butter underneath. Rub the rest all over the outside of the bird.
  4. Stuff the cavity with the onion quarters and lemon halves. Tie the legs together with twine and tuck the wing tips underneath.
  5. Set the turkey breast side up on a rack in a roasting pan. Pour 2 cups of stock into the pan so the drippings do not burn.
  6. Roast about 13 to 15 minutes per pound, until a thermometer in the thickest part of the thigh reads 165°F and the breast reads 160°F. Add more stock to the pan if it runs dry.
  7. Tent loosely with foil and rest at least 30 minutes before carving. Save every drop of pan drippings for gravy.
A frozen turkey needs about 24 hours in the fridge for every 4 to 5 pounds. Put it on a tray on the bottom shelf by Sunday night for a Thursday feast and you will never wrestle a half frozen bird again.
