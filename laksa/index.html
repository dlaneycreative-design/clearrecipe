Laksa

Springy rice noodles in a fragrant coconut curry broth with shrimp and chicken. Malaysia's spicy, creamy soup that eats like a hug.

Prep 15 minsCook 25 minsServes 4
#Malaysian / Singaporean#Soups

The whole recipe in 60 seconds

Laksa, in 5 ideas

  1. Coconut milk splits if boiled hard. Keep it at a gentle simmer for a silky, unified broth.
  2. Rinse the noodles cold. It stops the cooking and keeps them from turning to mush in the hot soup.
  3. Lime at the table. The squeeze of acid at the end lifts the whole rich bowl. Do not skip it.
  4. Bean sprouts stay crunchy. They go on top, never in the pot. The hot broth softens them just enough.
  5. Taste before serving. Laksa paste brands vary wildly in salt and heat. Fish sauce and sugar bring it into balance.

What you need

Ingredients

  • 8 oz dried rice noodles
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 3 tbsp laksa paste (store bought)
  • 1 (14 oz) can coconut milk
  • 4 cups chicken stock
  • 1 tbsp fish sauce
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 12 large shrimp, peeled and deveined
  • 2 cups bean sprouts
  • 2 hard-boiled eggs, halved
  • Lime wedges, for serving
  • Fresh cilantro, for serving

How to make it

Directions

  1. Cook the rice noodles according to the package. Rinse under cold water and divide among 4 deep bowls.
  2. Heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the laksa paste and fry 2 minutes, stirring, until deeply fragrant and darkened.
  3. Pour in the coconut milk and chicken stock. Bring to a simmer and cook 10 minutes.
  4. Stir in the fish sauce and sugar. Taste. It should be spicy, rich, and a little tangy.
  5. Add the shrimp and simmer 3 minutes, until pink and just cooked.
  6. Ladle the hot soup over the noodles in the bowls.
  7. Top each bowl with bean sprouts, half an egg, cilantro, and a lime wedge on the side.
Fry the paste first. Two minutes in hot oil until it darkens and smells incredible. That bloom is the soul of the soup.
