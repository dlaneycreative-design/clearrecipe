BBQ Baby Back Ribs

Low-and-slow smoked ribs with a caramelized bark and a sticky glaze. Tender enough to bite clean off the bone.

Prep 20 minsCook 5 to 6 hrsServes 4
#American#Mains

The whole recipe in 60 seconds

BBQ Baby Back Ribs, in 5 ideas

  1. 225F is the magic number. Too hot and the outside burns before the inside tenderizes. Low and slow renders the fat and melts the collagen.
  2. The wrap is called the Texas crutch. It powers through the stall (when the temp plateaus) by trapping steam. That is how you get tender without drying out.
  3. Do not sauce too early. Sugar burns. The glaze goes on in the last 30 minutes only, just long enough to set and caramelize.
  4. Let them rest. Fifteen minutes lets the juices redistribute. Cut too soon and they run out onto the board.
  5. No smoker, no problem. A kettle grill with indirect coals and wood chunks works. Even an oven at 225F with a drop of liquid smoke in the wrap gets you close.

What you need

Ingredients

  • 2 racks baby back ribs (about 4 lbs total)
  • 1/4 cup yellow mustard
  • 1/3 cup brown sugar
  • 2 tbsp paprika
  • 1 tbsp kosher salt
  • 1 tbsp black pepper
  • 1 tbsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp cayenne pepper
  • 1 cup apple juice, for spritzing
  • 1 cup BBQ sauce
  • 2 tbsp honey
  • Heavy-duty aluminum foil

How to make it

Directions

  1. Remove the thin membrane from the bone side of each rack. Slide a butter knife under it at one end, grip with a paper towel, and pull it off in one sheet.
  2. Rub a thin layer of mustard over both sides of the ribs. This is the binder that helps the rub stick.
  3. Mix the brown sugar, paprika, salt, pepper, garlic powder, and cayenne. Coat the ribs generously on all sides with the rub.
  4. Set up your smoker or grill for indirect heat at 225F. Add wood chunks (hickory or apple). Place the ribs bone-side down.
  5. Smoke for 3 hours, spritzing with apple juice every 45 minutes to keep the surface moist.
  6. Lay out two large sheets of foil. Place each rack on foil, drizzle with a little apple juice and honey, and wrap tightly. Return to the smoker for 2 hours.
  7. Unwrap the ribs and brush with BBQ sauce. Return to the heat unwrapped for 30 to 45 minutes to set the glaze and firm up the bark.
  8. Rest 15 minutes before slicing between the bones. The ribs are done when they bend and the bark cracks slightly.
The bend test never lies. Pick up a rack with tongs from the middle. If it bends nearly 90 degrees and the bark cracks, they are ready. If it stays stiff, give it more time.
