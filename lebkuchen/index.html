Lebkuchen

Soft German gingerbread: honey-sweetened spice cookies on crisp wafers, glazed and studded with almonds and candied citrus.

Prep 25 minsCook 18 minsServes 24

The whole recipe in 60 seconds

Lebkuchen, in 5 ideas

  1. The dough is sticky by design; damp hands make it manageable
  2. Oblaten wafers keep the bottoms crisp and are traditional
  3. The spice blend is flexible; add nutmeg or allspice to taste
  4. They taste better after 2 to 3 days as the flavors meld
  5. Dip half in dark chocolate for an extra-special version

What you need

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup honey
  • 1/2 cup brown sugar
  • 1/4 cup butter
  • 2 and 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 cup ground almonds
  • 2 teaspoons cinnamon, 1 teaspoon ginger, 1/2 teaspoon cloves, 1/2 teaspoon cardamom
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 2 eggs
  • 1/2 cup chopped candied orange peel
  • 24 round baking wafers (oblaten)
  • 1 cup powdered sugar mixed with 2 tablespoons water for glaze

How to make it

Directions

  1. Warm the honey, brown sugar, and butter in a saucepan until the sugar dissolves. Cool to lukewarm.
  2. Mix the flour, almonds, spices, and baking soda. Stir in the honey mixture, eggs, and candied peel to form a sticky dough. Rest 30 minutes.
  3. Place a wafer on a lined baking sheet and spread or press about 2 tablespoons of dough over it, doming slightly.
  4. Bake at 325 F for 15 to 18 minutes until firm and lightly browned.
  5. Brush the warm cookies with the sugar glaze and decorate with almond halves if you like.
  6. Cool completely and store airtight; they soften and improve over a few days.
Money saver. Buy honey in bulk at warehouse stores; lebkuchen uses a lot of it and small jars get expensive.
