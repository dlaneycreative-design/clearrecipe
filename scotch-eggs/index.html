Scotch Eggs

Crunchy golden breadcrumbs, savory sausage meat and a jammy soft-boiled egg in the middle. Pub food royalty, and even better eaten warm from the fryer.

Prep 25 minsCook 20 minsServes 6
#British#Starters

The whole recipe in 60 seconds

Scotch Eggs, in 5 ideas

  1. The yolk is the prize, so six minutes for the eggs plus an ice bath keeps them runny at the center.
  2. Season the sausage meat boldly: it has to carry the whole egg, so do not be shy with salt and pepper.
  3. Chill the wrapped eggs before breading so they hold their shape and the crumbs stick cleanly.
  4. Panko crumbs give a shattering crust that regular breadcrumbs cannot match.
  5. Eat them within a couple of hours for the best texture; the crust softens as they sit.

What you need

Ingredients

  • 6 large eggs, plus 2 eggs beaten for coating
  • 500g pork sausage meat
  • 1 tsp dried sage
  • 0.5 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 100g plain flour
  • 150g panko breadcrumbs
  • 1 litre vegetable oil, for deep frying
  • 1 tbsp Dijon mustard, to serve (optional)

How to make it

Directions

  1. Lower 6 eggs into boiling water and cook for exactly 6 minutes. Plunge into iced water, peel carefully and set aside.
  2. Mix the sausage meat with the sage, pepper and salt until evenly seasoned, then divide into 6 equal portions.
  3. Flatten one portion of sausage meat in your palm, wrap it evenly around an egg, and seal the join. Repeat for all 6.
  4. Roll each wrapped egg in flour, then beaten egg, then panko crumbs, pressing gently so the crumbs adhere.
  5. Chill the breaded eggs in the fridge for 15 minutes to firm up.
  6. Heat the oil to 170C in a deep heavy pan. Fry the eggs in batches for 6 to 7 minutes, turning, until deep golden all over.
  7. Drain on a wire rack over kitchen paper, rest for 5 minutes, and serve warm with mustard.
Use sausage meat from a value pack and a cheap bag of panko; they go much further per penny than buying individual sausages and fiddling with skins.
