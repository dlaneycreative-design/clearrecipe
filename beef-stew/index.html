Beef Stew

Chunks of beef braised until spoon tender with potatoes, carrots, and onions in a rich savory broth. A one pot hug.

Prep 20 minsCook 2 hrsServes 6
#American#Mains

The whole recipe in 60 seconds

Beef Stew, in 5 ideas

  1. Chuck is the ideal stew meat. Lean cuts turn dry and stringy with long cooking.
  2. Browning in batches is worth the time. Crowded meat steams instead of searing.
  3. The flour coating thickens the stew as it cooks, no slurry needed later.
  4. Tomato paste adds depth and a subtle sweetness that balances the rich beef.
  5. Cut the vegetables in large chunks so they survive the long simmer.

What you need

Ingredients

  • 2.5 lbs beef chuck, cut into 1.5 inch cubes
  • 1/3 cup flour mixed with 1 tsp salt and 1/2 tsp pepper
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 4 cups beef broth
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 tsp thyme and 2 bay leaves
  • 4 potatoes, cubed
  • 4 carrots, sliced
  • 1 cup frozen peas

How to make it

Directions

  1. Toss the beef with flour, salt, and pepper.
  2. Heat oil in a Dutch oven over medium high heat. Brown the beef in batches, about 8 minutes total. Remove to a plate.
  3. Add the onion and cook 5 minutes. Stir in the garlic, tomato paste, and thyme, cook 1 minute.
  4. Pour in the broth, scraping up the browned bits. Return the beef with the bay leaves.
  5. Cover and simmer on low 1 hour.
  6. Add the potatoes and carrots. Simmer 45 more minutes until the vegetables and beef are tender.
  7. Stir in the peas, cook 5 minutes. Remove bay leaves and serve.
Stew is better the second day. Make it ahead, refrigerate overnight, and reheat gently. The flavors deepen and the broth thickens.
