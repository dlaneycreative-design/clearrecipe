Black Forest Cake

Layers of dark chocolate sponge soaked in cherry syrup, billowy cream and a crown of sour cherries. A celebration cake in every sense.

Prep 45 mins (plus 2 hours chilling)Cook 35 minsServes 12
#German#Desserts

The whole recipe in 60 seconds

Black Forest Cake, in 5 ideas

  1. Brush the sponge layers generously with the cherry syrup; a dry layer is the most common failure with this cake.
  2. Chill the bowl and beaters before whipping the cream so it holds its shape for piping.
  3. Reserve your prettiest cherries for the top; appearance is half the joy of this cake.
  4. Use real sour cherries in syrup if you can find them; they taste far more authentic than fresh sweet cherries.
  5. Assemble cold and refrigerate for at least 2 hours so the layers settle and slice cleanly.

What you need

Ingredients

  • 6 large eggs
  • 180g caster sugar
  • 130g plain flour
  • 50g cocoa powder
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • For the filling: 600ml double cream
  • 60g icing sugar
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 350g sour cherries in syrup, drained (syrup reserved)
  • 3 tbsp kirsch (cherry brandy), optional
  • 100g dark chocolate, for shavings
  • 12 fresh or jarred cherries, for decorating

How to make it

Directions

  1. Heat the oven to 180C (160C fan). Line two 20cm round tins with parchment.
  2. Whisk the eggs and caster sugar for 8 minutes until thick, pale and tripled in volume. Sift over the flour, cocoa and baking powder and fold in gently.
  3. Divide between the tins and bake for 20 to 25 minutes until springy. Cool completely, then slice each cake horizontally into 2 layers.
  4. Mix the reserved cherry syrup with the kirsch if using. Brush every layer generously with the syrup.
  5. Whip the cream with the icing sugar and vanilla to firm peaks.
  6. Layer the cake with cream and drained sour cherries, finishing with a cream-covered top and sides.
  7. Decorate with chocolate shavings and the reserved cherries. Chill for at least 2 hours before serving.
Sour cherries in syrup from the international aisle are cheaper and more traditional than fresh cherries, and you get the syrup for soaking the sponge as a bonus.
