Madeleines

Buttery little shell-shaped cakes with crisp edges and tender lemon-scented centers. Best eaten still warm.

Prep 15 minsCook 12 minsServes 12
#French#Bakings

The whole recipe in 60 seconds

Madeleines, in 5 ideas

  1. The resting time is what creates the famous hump; cold batter hitting a hot pan rises dramatically.
  2. Do not overfill the molds; three-quarters full is the sweet spot.
  3. Brown the butter first for a nutty depth that elevates the whole cookie.
  4. They stale within hours, so bake only what you will eat.
  5. Dip half in melted chocolate for madeleines that look like a patisserie made them.

What you need

Ingredients

  • 2 eggs
  • 1/2 cup sugar
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • Zest of 1 lemon
  • 3/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 tsp baking powder
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1/2 cup butter, melted and cooled
  • Powdered sugar, for dusting

How to make it

Directions

  1. Whisk the eggs and sugar until thick, pale, and ribbon-like, about 5 minutes. Add vanilla and lemon zest.
  2. Fold in the flour, baking powder, and salt gently. Drizzle in the melted butter and fold just to combine.
  3. Rest the batter in the fridge at least 1 hour, or overnight.
  4. Heat the oven to 375F. Butter and flour a madeleine pan generously.
  5. Fill each shell three-quarters full. Bake 10 to 12 minutes until golden with the signature hump.
  6. Cool 2 minutes, unmold, and dust with powdered sugar. Serve warm.
Money saver. A madeleine pan is a small investment that pays off forever. Silicone versions release the cakes effortlessly.
