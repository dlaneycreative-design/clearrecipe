Cranberry Sauce

Bright, tart, and just sweet enough, with a hint of orange. It takes 15 minutes and beats the canned kind by a mile.

Prep 5 minsCook 15 minsServes 8 to 10
#American#Sides

The whole recipe in 60 seconds

Cranberry Sauce, in 5 ideas

  1. The berries tell you when it is done. Once they pop, the natural pectin does the thickening.
  2. Orange juice and zest brighten the tart berries without extra sugar.
  3. This sauce is better made ahead. A day in the fridge deepens the flavor.
  4. Leave some berries whole for a rustic texture, or mash them all for a smooth sauce.
  5. A pinch of salt sounds odd but it rounds out the sweetness beautifully.

What you need

Ingredients

  • 12 oz fresh cranberries
  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup orange juice
  • 1/2 cup water
  • 1 tsp orange zest
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • Pinch of salt

How to make it

Directions

  1. Rinse the cranberries and pick out any that are soft or shriveled.
  2. Combine the sugar, orange juice, and water in a medium saucepan and bring to a simmer, stirring until the sugar dissolves.
  3. Add the cranberries, cinnamon stick, and salt. Return to a gentle boil.
  4. Cook 10 to 12 minutes, stirring now and then, until most of the berries have popped and the sauce looks glossy.
  5. Remove from the heat and stir in the orange zest. Pull out the cinnamon stick.
  6. Let the sauce cool to room temperature. It will set into a soft jam as it cools.
  7. Chill at least 2 hours before serving so the flavors settle and the texture firms up.
Make it up to a week early and keep it in a sealed jar in the fridge. One less pot on the stove on Thanksgiving Day is a real gift to the cook.
