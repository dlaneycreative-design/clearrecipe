Gorditas

Thick griddled corn cakes split and stuffed with savory fillings. Little fat ones with big flavor, straight from the comal.

Prep 20 minsCook 20 minsServes 4
#Mexican#Mains

The whole recipe in 60 seconds

Gorditas, in 5 ideas

  1. Gordita means little fat one, a term of endearment for these chubby cakes
  2. The steam rest after griddling is what makes them puff and stay tender
  3. Masa dough should feel like soft play dough, not sticky or crumbly
  4. Any taco filling works; beans and cheese is the classic budget option
  5. A cast iron skillet is the perfect home substitute for a comal

What you need

Ingredients

  • 2 cups masa harina
  • 1.5 cups warm water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1/2 tsp baking powder
  • 2 tbsp lard or oil
  • 2 cups filling (carnitas, beans, or chicharron)
  • Shredded lettuce, crema, and salsa, for serving

How to make it

Directions

  1. Mix masa harina, water, salt, baking powder, and lard into a soft dough. Rest 10 minutes.
  2. Form into 8 thick discs, about 4 inches wide and 1/2 inch thick.
  3. Cook on a hot dry comal or skillet 4 minutes per side until speckled.
  4. Cover with a towel for 5 minutes to steam and soften.
  5. Split each gordita partway with a knife to form a pocket.
  6. Stuff with filling and top with lettuce, crema, and salsa.
Money saver. Masa harina is cheap and lasts forever in the pantry; one bag makes dozens of gorditas, sopes, and tortillas.
