Tzatziki

Thick Greek yogurt with grated cucumber, garlic, lemon, and dill. Cool, garlicky, and the perfect partner for grilled meats.

Prep 15 mins plus 1 hr restingCook noneServes 8 (about 2 cups)
#Greece#Sides

The whole recipe in 60 seconds

Tzatziki, in 5 ideas

  1. Full-fat yogurt only. Nonfat yogurt is thin and tangy in the wrong way. The fat is what makes it rich and spoonable.
  2. Rest it before serving. Freshly mixed tzatziki tastes harsh from the raw garlic. An hour in the fridge rounds everything out.
  3. Mince the garlic fine. Big chunks of raw garlic are aggressive. A fine mince distributes evenly.
  4. Dill is classic, mint works too. Purists use dill. Mint is common in some regions. Do not use both.
  5. Serve it cold. Room temperature tzatziki loses its refreshing snap. Keep it chilled until the moment it hits the table.

What you need

Ingredients

  • 2 cups full-fat Greek yogurt
  • 1 large cucumber
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil, plus more to serve
  • 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice
  • 2 tbsp chopped fresh dill
  • 1 tsp salt

How to make it

Directions

  1. Grate the cucumber on the large holes of a box grater. Toss with half the salt and rest 10 minutes in a colander.
  2. Squeeze the cucumber completely dry in a clean kitchen towel, wringing hard. You want it almost powdery. This is the most important step.
  3. Stir the yogurt, cucumber, garlic, olive oil, lemon juice, dill, and remaining salt together in a bowl.
  4. Taste and adjust. It should be tangy, garlicky, and well salted. Add more lemon or salt until it tastes bright.
  5. Rest at least 1 hour in the fridge. The garlic mellows and the flavors marry.
  6. Serve cold with a thread of olive oil and a pinch of dill on top.
Wring the cucumber like you mean it. Watery cucumber makes watery tzatziki. Squeeze until nothing more comes out, then squeeze again.
