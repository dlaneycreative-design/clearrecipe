    #Persian #Main #Medium

    Tahdig

    Persian rice with the prize at the bottom: a shattering golden crust of saffron rice, flipped out of the pot in one piece, over fluffy, separate long grains.

    Prep 15 mins, plus 30 mins soakingCook 1 hrServes 6

    The whole recipe in 60 seconds

    Tahdig, in 5 ideas

    1. Tahdig means bottom of the pot, and in Persian cooking the crust is the honor. The cook breaks it and shares it around the table.
    2. Parboiling then steaming is the Persian method. The rice goes into the pot already half cooked, so the grains finish long and separate.
    3. Yogurt and saffron in the crust layer are the insurance policy. Tang, color and a crust that fries evenly instead of scorching.
    4. The towel-wrapped lid is essential. It drinks the steam that would otherwise rain back down and soften the crust.
    5. The flip is a moment of faith. Dry pot, hot crust, one confident turn onto the platter, and listen for the crackle.

    What you need

    Ingredients

    • 3 cups basmati rice
    • 1/4 cup fine sea salt, for the boiling water
    • A large pinch of saffron threads
    • 1/3 cup plain yogurt
    • 1 egg yolk (optional)
    • 4 tbsp butter or oil
    • 2 tbsp oil, for the pot

    How to make it

    Method

    1. Rinse the rice until the water runs clear and soak in salted water for 30 minutes. Steep the saffron in 1/4 cup of hot water.
    2. Boil the rice in well-salted water for 6 to 7 minutes, until the grains are soft at the edges with a firm core. Drain and rinse briefly.
    3. Stir 2 cups of the parboiled rice with the yogurt, egg yolk if using, and half the saffron water.
    4. Heat the oil in a nonstick pot until hot. Spread the yogurt rice in an even layer in the bottom and press gently.
    5. Mound the rest of the rice on top in a pyramid. Poke a few steam holes with a spoon handle and drizzle the butter and remaining saffron water over the top.
    6. Cover with a towel-wrapped lid and cook on medium-high for 5 minutes, then the lowest heat for 45 minutes, until the crust is deep golden; you will smell it turn toasty.
    7. Rest 5 minutes. Loosen the edges and flip the rice onto a platter in one motion. Serve the crust in proud shards over the rice; if the crust stays behind, scrape it out and arrange it on top like the treasure it is.
    A good nonstick pot is the honest cook's friend here; a well-seasoned cast iron pan works too, with an extra spoon of oil.
