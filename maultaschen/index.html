Maultaschen

Swabian stuffed pasta pockets: tender dough wrapped around seasoned pork, spinach, and onion, simmered in clear broth.

Prep 1 hourCook 20 minsServes 6

The whole recipe in 60 seconds

Maultaschen, in 5 ideas

  1. Roll the dough as thin as you can; thick dough makes gummy pockets
  2. Press out all the air when sealing so they do not burst in the broth
  3. Legend says monks invented them to hide meat during Lent, hence the nickname Herrgottsbescheisserle
  4. Sliced and fried in butter the next day, they are arguably even better
  5. Freeze uncooked on a tray, then bag them for quick meals

What you need

Ingredients

  • 3 cups all-purpose flour
  • 3 eggs plus 3 tablespoons water and 1 teaspoon salt, for the dough
  • 1 lb ground pork
  • 1/2 lb cooked spinach, squeezed very dry
  • 1 onion, finely diced
  • 1 egg
  • 1/2 cup breadcrumbs
  • 1 teaspoon salt, 1/2 teaspoon pepper, 1/4 teaspoon nutmeg
  • 8 cups beef or chicken broth, for simmering

How to make it

Directions

  1. Make the dough: mix flour, eggs, water, and salt, knead 8 minutes until smooth, wrap and rest 30 minutes.
  2. For the filling, combine the pork, spinach, onion, egg, breadcrumbs, and seasonings, mixing well.
  3. Roll the dough thin on a floured surface and cut into 4-inch squares.
  4. Place 2 tablespoons of filling on each square, brush the edges with water, fold over, and press firmly to seal.
  5. Bring the broth to a gentle simmer and cook the maultaschen in batches for 12 to 15 minutes until they float and the filling is cooked through.
  6. Serve in the hot broth with chives, or slice and pan-fry the leftovers in butter.
Money saver. Use frozen chopped spinach, thawed and squeezed dry; it is cheaper than fresh and works perfectly here.
