Confit de Canard

Duck legs cured with salt and herbs, then cooked low and slow in their own fat until impossibly tender. A French bistro legend.

Prep 24 hrs (cure)Cook 3 hrsServes 4
#French#Mains

The whole recipe in 60 seconds

Confit de Canard, in 5 ideas

  1. The salt cure seasons the meat and draws out moisture for better texture
  2. Cooking at very low heat breaks down connective tissue without drying the meat
  3. Confit keeps for weeks submerged in fat in the fridge
  4. Crisping the skin at the end gives the contrast of crunchy and silky
  5. Save the leftover fat; it is liquid gold for roasting potatoes

What you need

Ingredients

  • 4 duck legs (thigh and drumstick)
  • 1/4 cup kosher salt
  • 4 cloves garlic, crushed
  • 2 bay leaves, crumbled
  • 1 tbsp fresh thyme leaves
  • 1 tsp black peppercorns, cracked
  • 4 cups duck fat, melted

How to make it

Directions

  1. Rub the duck legs with salt, garlic, bay leaves, thyme, and peppercorns.
  2. Refrigerate uncovered 24 hours to cure.
  3. Rinse the cure off and pat the legs completely dry.
  4. Place in a deep pot and cover fully with melted duck fat.
  5. Cook in a 225 F oven for 3 hours until the meat pulls easily from the bone.
  6. Cool in the fat, then crisp the skin in a hot skillet before serving.
Money saver. Duck fat is reusable. Strain it after cooking and freeze it for the next batch or for the best roast potatoes of your life.
