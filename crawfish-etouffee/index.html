Crawfish Etouffee

A rich Cajun stew of crawfish tails smothered in a dark roux gravy with the holy trinity of onion, celery, and bell pepper. Ladled over rice, it is Louisiana in a bowl.

Prep 25 minsCook 50 minsServes 6
#American#Mains

The whole recipe in 60 seconds

Crawfish Etouffee, in 5 ideas

  1. Do not rush the roux, a pale roux gives pale flavor. Peanut butter color is the target.
  2. The onion, celery, and bell pepper are the Cajun holy trinity, never skip them.
  3. Add the stock gradually so the gravy stays smooth instead of lumpy.
  4. Crawfish tails are already cooked, so add them last and heat gently to keep them tender.
  5. Etouffee means smothered, the crawfish should swim in gravy, not sit in a thick paste.

What you need

Ingredients

  • 900g cooked crawfish tails, peeled
  • 80g unsalted butter
  • 60g plain flour
  • 1 large onion, 2 celery stalks, 1 green bell pepper, all diced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 500ml seafood or chicken stock
  • 1 tbsp Cajun seasoning, 1 tsp paprika, 1/2 tsp dried thyme, 2 bay leaves
  • 4 spring onions, sliced
  • 2 tbsp chopped fresh parsley
  • 600g cooked long-grain rice, to serve

How to make it

Directions

  1. Melt the butter in a heavy pot over medium heat. Whisk in the flour and cook, stirring constantly, 20 to 25 minutes until the roux turns the color of peanut butter.
  2. Add the onion, celery, and bell pepper to the roux. Cook 8 minutes, stirring often, until the vegetables soften.
  3. Stir in the garlic, Cajun seasoning, paprika, and thyme. Cook 1 minute until fragrant.
  4. Pour in the stock slowly, whisking to keep it smooth. Add the bay leaves and simmer 15 minutes until the gravy thickens enough to coat a spoon.
  5. Fold in the crawfish tails and simmer 8 to 10 minutes more, just until heated through.
  6. Remove the bay leaves. Stir in the spring onions and parsley.
  7. Spoon generously over hot cooked rice and serve.
Frozen crawfish tails from the seafood freezer aisle are much cheaper than fresh and work perfectly in a stew like this.
