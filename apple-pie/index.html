Apple Pie

Flaky golden crust wrapped around tender cinnamon-spiced apples, the all-American dessert that fills the kitchen with the smell of fall. Best served warm with a scoop of vanilla ice cream melting over the top.

Prep 30 minsCook 55 minsServes 8
#American#Desserts

The whole recipe in 60 seconds

Apple Pie, in 5 ideas

  1. Mix two apple varieties: a tart one like Granny Smith plus a sweet one like Honeycrisp gives the best flavor and texture.
  2. Slice the apples uniformly, about 1/4 inch thick, so they cook evenly and none stay crunchy.
  3. Mounding the filling high is fine: apples shrink as they bake, so pile them up.
  4. The two-temperature bake gives a crisp browned crust without overbaking the fruit.
  5. If the edges brown too fast, cover the rim with strips of aluminum foil halfway through baking.

What you need

Ingredients

  • 2 pie crusts (one 9-inch bottom, one for the top)
  • 6 cups thinly sliced peeled apples (about 6 medium Granny Smith or Honeycrisp)
  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 1 tablespoon butter, cut into small pieces
  • 1 large egg, beaten with 1 tablespoon milk (for egg wash)

How to make it

Directions

  1. Preheat the oven to 425 degrees F (220 C). Fit the bottom pie crust into a 9-inch pie dish, letting the excess hang over the edge.
  2. In a large bowl, toss the sliced apples with lemon juice, sugar, flour, cinnamon, nutmeg, and salt until every slice is coated.
  3. Pile the apple mixture into the crust, mounding it slightly in the center. Dot the top with the small pieces of butter.
  4. Lay the second crust over the filling. Trim, fold, and crimp the edges to seal, then cut 4 or 5 slits in the top for steam.
  5. Brush the top crust with the egg wash and bake for 20 minutes at 425 F, then reduce the heat to 375 F (190 C) and bake 30 to 35 minutes more, until deep golden and bubbling.
  6. Cool on a wire rack for at least 2 hours before slicing so the filling sets.
Buy bagged apples in bulk when they go on sale in the fall and freeze extra slices; frozen apples work perfectly in pie and cost a fraction of off-season prices.
